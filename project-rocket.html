Aerodynamic Design for Stratospheric Reach

As part of a 20+ person multidisciplinary engineering team, I led the aerodynamic design of a hybrid-fueled rocket targeting the stratosphere. My responsibility covered the full outer shell geometry, nose cone selection, fin configuration, and CFD-based optimization of the drag profile.

My Scope

  • Drag profile evaluation across subsonic and transonic flow regimes
  • Nose cone profile analysis — elliptical vs conical geometries
  • Fin design and stability optimization
  • CFD simulation in ANSYS for flow field characterization
  • Design of experiments for wind tunnel validation at Cambridge University

CFD Analysis

Performed detailed CFD simulations to evaluate flow characteristics around the rocket body at multiple Mach numbers. Analyzed density, temperature, pressure, and Mach number contours to identify drag contributors and optimize geometry.

  • Evaluated multiple nose cone profiles (elliptical vs conical) for drag performance
  • Analyzed density, temperature, pressure, and Mach number contours
  • Designed fin configuration optimized for stability across flight envelope
  • Future-proofed validated model for rotation characteristics analysis

Wind Tunnel Validation

Designed and executed experiments at Cambridge University's wind tunnel facility to validate CFD predictions. Achieved validation across four separate experimental methods — a rigorous confirmation of the simulation accuracy.

  • Schlieren flow visualization — observing shock waves and flow structures
  • Oil surface flow testing — identifying boundary layer separation
  • Pressure probes — quantifying pressure distributions
  • Pressure Sensitive Paint (PSP) — full-field surface pressure mapping

Result

Achieved CFD validation across all four experimental methods, confirming the accuracy of the simulation models and providing a validated aerodynamic dataset for the full rocket design team.
